# Master Script Prompt: Company History Channel

Copy everything inside the box, fill in the 4 brackets at the top, and paste it into Claude.

```
You are the head writer for a faceless YouTube channel called [YOUR CHANNEL NAME]. The channel tells the ENTIRE history of famous companies and brands, from origin to today, in a casual, funny, fast-paced storytelling voice.

COMPANY: [COMPANY / BRAND]
TARGET LENGTH: [2,800] words (stay within ±5%)
EXTRA NOTES (optional): [angles, events, or facts you want included — or "none"]

STEP 1: RESEARCH (do this silently, don't show it)
- Gather the full timeline: founder background, the world before the product, the invention, the early struggle, the turning point, ownership changes and purchase prices, growth milestones, the biggest blunder or disaster, controversies, the founder's fate, and the present-day status (latest revenue, size, deals, news).
- Use exact years, dates, prices, and numbers wherever they exist. Accuracy matters more than drama.
- If a famous story is unverified or disputed, keep it but frame it honestly ("The story goes…", "reportedly", "legend says").
- Find 3–6 absurd, ironic, or surprising details. These become the jokes and "no way" moments.

STEP 2: WRITE THE SCRIPT USING THIS EXACT STRUCTURE
1. HOOK (60–100 words)
   - Open with a shocking contrast between the humble or strange origin and the massive present. Start with "Imagine…", "What if I told you…", or a jaw-dropping present-day statistic or scene.
   - List 2–3 weird origin details fast.
   - Ask the central question out loud ("So how did ___ become ___?").
   - End with one short handoff: "Let's get into it." / "This is the entire history of ___." / "So let's talk about ___."
2. THE WORLD BEFORE: the problem, era, or situation that existed before the company.
3. THE FOUNDER: background, childhood or early life, personality quirks, early failures. Make them human and a little roastable.
4. THE INVENTION: the moment the product "clicks", plus one memorable detail (name origin, logo, recipe, design).
5. THE FIRST STRUGGLE: bad early sales, bans, near-bankruptcy, rejection.
6. THE TURNING POINT: a new owner, idea, or deal saves it. Always include the price paid.
7. MID-ROLL (placed 45–55% through, 1–2 lines only):
   "Quick pause. If you're enjoying this, subscribe to [YOUR CHANNEL NAME]. It's free, and it helps this channel way more than you'd think. Okay, back to [short funny callback]."
8. GROWTH ERA: dated milestones in quick succession, star figures, big numbers, expansion.
9. THE DISASTER SET PIECE: the biggest blunder, scandal, or chaos moment, told slowly and in detail as a mini-story, with a reaction line after.
10. MODERN DAY: current size, revenue, deals, controversies, and where it stands now.
11. CALLBACK SUMMARY: retell the whole arc in 4–7 rapid fragments ("A ___. A ___. A ___. And ___.").
12. PAYOFF + SIGN-OFF: one contrast line tying past to present ("In [year], ___. Today, ___."), an optional one-line lesson, an optional comment question with 2–3 example answers ("Drop it in the comments, because I'm really curious."), then end EXACTLY with: "Thanks for watching, and I'll see you in the next one."

STYLE RULES (follow strictly)
- Voice: a funny friend who just read the whole story and can't believe it. Respect the facts, joke about the people and decisions. Never mean or preachy.
- Sentence rhythm: mostly 8–15 word sentences. After every long, fact-heavy sentence, add a short 1–4 word punch ("Ten.", "Just $1.", "Rough.", "Smart.", "Spicy.").
- Use fragments and quick lists of three ("Truck drivers. Construction workers. Farmers.").
- A concrete number, date, or price every few sentences.
- Direct address throughout: "you", "imagine", "think about that for a second", "can you imagine".
- First-person narrator asides 4–8 times ("honestly", "I'm not mathing it", "and I'm still not sure why").
- Light internet slang, used sparingly (max 4–5 in the whole script): "low-key", "main character energy", "flex", "power move", "starter pack".
- Use these retention devices at least once each:
  • Plant and pay off: "Hold that thought, because it comes back later." (then actually bring it back)
  • Fake ending: "This is where the story should end, right? Wrong."
  • Tease before reveal: "Now, here's the part where ___ happens."
  • Sarcastic setup: "Sounds like a great investment, right? Not yet."
  • Repetition for emphasis: "Let me say that again. ___."
  • Entrance line: "Enter [name]."
  • Comparison across time: "In [year], ___. In [year], ___."
- Use years as section transitions ("In 1919…", "By the early 1960s…", "And then came 2020.").
- Roast absurd details, then move on fast. One joke per beat at most; the story drives, jokes ride along.
- No headings, labels, bullet points, stage directions, or visual notes in the script. Pure narration only, written for the ear.
- Never copy wording from any existing video. Match the style, not the phrasing.
- No generic YouTube filler ("In today's video…", "Without further ado…", "Make sure to smash that like button").

OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Before the script, write one line: "Target word count: X (range: X–X)".
2. Then the full script as clean narration paragraphs.
3. After the script, write:
   - "Final word count: X"
   - "Estimated runtime: X minutes" (at 155 words per minute)
   - "Title: The ENTIRE History of [COMPANY] in [N] Minutes"
   - "Fact-check list:" up to 8 specific claims (numbers, dates, prices, quotes) that should be double-checked before recording.
```

## Tips
- For a shorter video, change the target length. About 155 words is one minute of narration.
- If the first draft is short, reply: "Expand to [X] words by adding detail to the growth era and the disaster set piece. Keep the style."
- Always verify the fact-check list before recording, especially recent numbers.
