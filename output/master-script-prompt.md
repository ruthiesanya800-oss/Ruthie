# Master Script Prompt: Feminine Self-Improvement Channel

Copy everything below the line. Fill in the four fields in [BRACKETS], then paste it into Claude.

---

You are the head scriptwriter for a faceless YouTube channel in the feminine self-improvement niche (mindset, discipline, self-worth, boundaries, ambition, "main character" energy). Write ONE complete, original voiceover script for the video below.

VIDEO TOPIC: [TOPIC, e.g. "The Quiet Discipline of a Woman Who Wins"]
NARRATOR NAME: [e.g. Elara]
TARGET WORD COUNT: [default 3,150. Stay within ±5%]
CHORUS LINE (optional): [a short repeated line, or "write one for me"]

## WHO IT'S FOR
Women aged 18 to 30 who feel stuck, behind, "too much" or not enough. They watch late at night and want permission and a push more than information. They are drawn to the "that girl" or quiet-luxury life, and they respond to both practical habits and spiritual or self-concept framing.

## FORMAT (non-negotiable)
- Build the script as 30 to 46 short, self-contained SEGMENTS, each 30 to 90 seconds when spoken (about 40 to 180 words).
- Put the marker [cut] on its own line between every segment. It marks a hard visual cut with a music swell and is not read aloud.
- Do not write an intro, a "welcome back", channel plugs, chapter titles, a recap or an outro.
- Open cold on a punchline. End on a command.
- Each segment must make sense on its own, like a standalone reel, but every segment serves the topic.
- Pacing is about 3 words per second, so 3,150 words is about 17 minutes.

## SEGMENT ANATOMY
Use this four-step shape inside most segments:
1. CLAIM: a short, absolute statement or provocation. No warm-up.
2. REFRAME: "It's not X. It's Y." / "You don't need X. You need Y."
3. PROOF: one of a numbered list, a relatable micro-scene, a quick personal confession, a contrast between how "she" thinks and how "you" think, a simple psychology explanation, a metaphor, or a short exercise.
4. COMMAND: an imperative closing line that people would screenshot.

## ROTATE THESE SEGMENT TYPES
Use at least 8 different types across the script and never put the same type twice in a row.
- Tough-love truth ("Here's the cold truth…")
- Soft reassurance ("Babe, you're not that little girl anymore.")
- Psychology explainer (how habits, guilt and identity work, in plain words)
- Numbered list ("Here are the things I've noticed about women who…", "Here are the things she quietly stopped doing.")
- Personal confession from the narrator (honest, a little vulnerable, first person)
- Micro-scene the viewer recognises (texting back at midnight, cancelling on yourself, the gym bag by the door)
- Metaphor segment (your life is a house, a movie, a garden, a witness)
- Interactive exercise ("Think about the last time you… Now picture the version of you who…")
- Practical "start this week" steps
- Identity and aura segment ("There's a kind of woman who walks into a room and…")
- Circle and relationships segment (friends who clap, dating, boundaries)
- Money, body or attention segment (independence, caring for your body, protecting your focus)
- One-line punch segment (a single quotable sentence between two cuts)

## EMOTIONAL ARC (in waves, not a straight line)
Permission → confrontation → psychology and identity → practical habits → tenderness → expansion and vision → final rallying call.
Alternate intensity: follow a hard-hitting segment with a softer one.

## CHORUS AND CALLBACKS
- Write one short chorus line (8 to 14 words) that captures the topic, and use it as its own segment 3 to 4 times: once at the start, twice spread through the middle, and once in the final segment.
- Bring back 1 or 2 other key phrases later in the script, almost word for word.

## VOICE AND STYLE RULES
- Speak to "you" in almost every sentence. Use "we" for the community ("we're not doing that anymore"). Use "she" and "the woman who…" as the aspirational mirror.
- Use pet names sparingly (2 to 4 times in the whole script): babe, girl, my girls.
- Sentence rhythm: bursts of short sentences. Use anaphora (a repeated phrase at the start of 3 lines in a row). Follow a long, flowing sentence with a 2 to 4 word punch. Fragments are allowed.
- Tone: big-sister intimacy mixed with coach intensity. Confident and never hedging. Blunt, then tender.
- Use curiosity gaps and open loops: "Here's what nobody tells you…", "And this is the part most people get wrong…", "The real reason isn't what you think."
- Triggers to use: validation, gentle shame used as fuel (never cruel), fear of regret, belonging, quiet rebellion, fear of your own potential, a calm sense of destiny.
- Keep it inclusive and real: no body-shaming, no hating men, no wealth-flexing, no medical or financial promises.
- Never put words in a real person's mouth. Use no real names, celebrities or brands, and no direct quotes from books or other creators.
- 100% original wording. Match the style, never copy phrasing from existing videos.
- Write for the ear. No hashtags, emojis, headings, stage directions or bullet points inside the script.

## OUTPUT FORMAT
1. One line: "Target: X words (range A–B)".
2. The full script, with segments separated by [cut] on its own line.
3. One line: "Final word count: X (not counting [cut] markers)". Count accurately. If you're outside the range, add or trim whole segments before you answer.
4. After the script, 3 title options (under 60 characters, in the style "The Psychology of…", "The Art of…", "This Video Will Find You When…") and 3 one-to-two-word thumbnail text options.

## SELF-CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER (do it silently)
- Is every segment self-contained, and does each start with a hook in its first sentence?
- Does the chorus appear 3 to 4 times, and does the script end on a command?
- Are there at least 8 segment types, with no type twice in a row?
- Is there no intro, no outro and no plug?
- Is the word count within ±5% of the target?
- Is every line original and quotable, with nothing generic like "In today's video" or "Let's dive in"?
