## STEP 1: SPLIT INTO BEATS
- Speech pace is 3 words per second. One beat is at most 5 seconds, so at most 15 words (aim for 8 to 13).
- Split at sentence ends first, then at commas. Never split in the middle of a phrase.
- Very short sentences (under 6 words) can be merged with the next one if the beat stays at or under 13 words.
- Clip length = beat words ÷ 3, rounded up, with a minimum of 2 seconds and a maximum of 5.
- Cover EVERY word of the script, in order. Do not skip, summarise or merge segments.
- A [cut] marker means a new segment starts. Number the beats continuously through the whole script.

## STEP 3: SEGMENT LOOK
For each segment, decide on one each of:
- **Location:** a real, specific, aspirational place. European city apartment, balcony, café, rooftop bar, gallery, train, beach, misty lake, candlelit bistro, gym at dawn, flower market and so on.
- **Lighting:** golden hour backlight, soft dawn through sheer curtains, warm practical lamps at night, blue hour with city glow, candlelight, rainy window light, phone-screen glow.
- **Mood:** 2 to 3 words, for example "quiet resolve", "tense, then liberated", "tender introspection".
Match the look to the segment's emotion: confrontation goes darker, cooler and more low-key; liberation goes golden, open and outdoors; intimacy goes to close lamplight.

## STEP 4: CHORUS SHOT
Every time the script's chorus line comes back (the repeated line), cut to the SAME signature location: a Milan rooftop terrace at blue hour with city lights below and wind moving through her hair and clothes. Each time, use whichever woman is next in the rotation, so the place stays the same and the face changes. It should feel like the message is being passed from woman to woman. Vary the camera angle each time. The final beat of the video should end on an open, hopeful, golden image, such as a sunrise beach run.

## STEP 5: CHOOSING THE SHOT FOR EACH BEAT
Show the meaning of the line visually; never literally act out the words. Rotate shot types and don't use the same one 3 times in a row:
- Wide establishing shot, 24mm, slow push-in. Use it to open a segment.
- Medium shot, 35mm, gentle handheld drift.
- Medium close-up, 50mm, slow dolly-in.
- Close-up on the face, 85mm, very slow push-in with a soft rack focus. Use it for emotional lines.
- Extreme close-up or macro (eyes, tears, a thumb on a screen) with subtle focus breathing.
- Insert shot of hands and objects (phone face-down, journal, keys, gym bag, coffee, savings jar). Use it for concrete actions.
- Over-the-shoulder shot with a blurred foreground, slow lateral slide. Use it for scenes about other people or screens.
- Low angle, slow tilt up. Use it for empowerment lines.
- High angle, slow overhead drift. Use it for exhaustion or being stuck.
- Side tracking shot as she walks. Use it for momentum lines.
- Shot from behind, steady follow. Use it to end a segment or walk into the light.
- Profile shot, slow arc. Use it for reflective lines.
Women are always caught mid-moment and never pose or talk to the camera.

## STEP 6: WRITE EACH VIDEO PROMPT (fully standalone)
Every prompt must make sense alone and follow this template:

"Photorealistic cinematic video clip, [N] seconds, 16:9, 24fps, 35mm film look with fine grain, modern coming-of-age drama aesthetic. Subject: [full woman description], wearing [segment outfit]. Action: [one clear, simple, physically realistic action], moving naturally and unhurried, a candid unposed moment. Setting: [specific location]. Lighting: [lighting]. Camera: [shot type and lens]; [camera movement]. Ambient motion: [1–3 small background movements: breeze in hair, dust motes in light, candle flicker, rain on glass, city bokeh twinkling, water glinting, background figures moving softly out of focus]. Mood: [mood]. Warm amber and honey tones against teal and navy shadows, muted soft-contrast orange-and-teal grade, shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh, realistic skin and hair detail, natural physics, no sudden cuts, no warping, no morphing faces, no extra fingers, no speaking to camera, no text, no logos, no watermark."

Rules:
- Use one action per clip. AI video handles simple motion best.
- No on-screen words, no readable screens (phones and laptops are seen from an angle or face-down), no real people, no celebrities, no brands, no copyrighted characters.
- Inclusive, tasteful and never sexualised. Bodies are shown with care, not judgement.
